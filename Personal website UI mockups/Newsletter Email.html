Statistical Technicians

The case for teaching statistics as reasoning, not procedure

9 min read

[ Plate image — swap in a hosted https:// URL, 1120×700, alt text describing the figure ]

Fig. 1 — Coverage of the nominal 95% interval, 10,000 simulations.

A student who can run a t-test and cannot say what it assumes has learned a ritual, not a method. We produce a great many of them, and then we are surprised when the published literature reads like a catalogue of rituals performed correctly on the wrong questions.

The fault is in the sequencing. We teach the machinery first and the reasoning last, if at all — as though the point of inference were to arrive at a number rather than to decide what the number is allowed to mean.

  Every procedure is a compressed argument. Teach the argument and the procedure follows; teach the procedure and the argument never arrives.
